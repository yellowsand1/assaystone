Your agent's tests pass.
Did it delete the failing test?

Assaystone turns one unit of agent work into a certificate: pass, fail, or a human must look — with the evidence, the check that decided it, what that check is worth, and the cost of deciding. It runs on a live git worktree, a recorded trace, or a runtime action. Every check declares what it cannot see; every decision can be re-checked offline.

From episode to certificate

Check once. Re-check anywhere.

Start with a worktree or a transcript you already have. No model judge, hosted control plane, or vendor migration required.

01

Audit the run

Recover the files and actions the agent actually touched. Surface test tampering, forbidden edits, unsafe actions, and unsupported success claims.

02

Turn findings into a contract

The contract is versioned with your code. Every verifier declares its evidence source, cost, layer, and independence.

03

Hold the certificate

A hash-addressed record of the decision: evidence, the check that decided, the check that could not, the assurance gap, and the cost. Verify it offline, without the agent.

04

Prove the checks

Recall on a labelled corpus, resistance to an agent that knows the contract, coverage of the declared defects, and cost. A check without a clean case, a defective case, and a measured false-alarm rate does not ship.

Check a worktree
$ assaystone check --base origin/main \
    --certificate ./certs
$ assaystone certificate verify ./certs/<id>.certificate.json

Four questions, one page

Measure the verifier,
not just the agent.

Every check declares what it catches and what it cannot see. Then the benchmark measures the whole set.

01

Recall

Did it catch a known defect? The Failure Zoo scores every verifier against labelled cases.

02

Resistance

Can an agent that knows the contract pass without doing the work? Mechanical attacks, action lineage, and semantic escapes are reported separately.

03

Coverage

Which declared defect classes are decided by an independent check, which are weak, and which are not covered at all.

04

Independence

A low-independence failure cannot decide alone. It escalates unless an independent check confirms it.

05

Calibration

Every certificate says how many of its checks have been measured, and the gap. A check with no data is unmeasured — it never gets a number it did not earn.

Prove the checks
$ assaystone prove
recall=100%  resistance=85%  false_alarms=0
Re-check a decision
$ assaystone certificate show ./certs/<id>.certificate.json
$ assaystone certificate verify ./certs/<id>.certificate.json

Designed for production trust

Deterministic first.
Escalation is a feature.

01

Verify artifacts, not self-reports

“All tests pass” is a claim. Exit codes, files, and independent checks are evidence.

02

Compose, don’t replace

Keep LangSmith, Langfuse, or OpenTelemetry. Assaystone owns the acceptance boundary.

03

Test the verifiers

Mutation testing measures recall. The Goodhart suite measures whether an agent can satisfy the check without doing the work.

04

Name your blind spots

Every verifier declares what it cannot see. A check that cannot name its blind spots is claiming to be an oracle.

05

Fail closed only when justified

Pass, fail, or escalate. Unknown evidence never quietly becomes success.
