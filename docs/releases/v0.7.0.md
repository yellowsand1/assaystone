# v0.7.0 — Chained lifecycle stages

Released 2026-11-25.

0.6.0 introduced the acceptance plane. 0.7.0 makes the stages a chain instead of
three independent gates: production can require evidence that canary actually
passed, under the same acceptance rules, and the trail is verified before it is
trusted.

## Highlights

- **`after` in a stage policy.** `"production": {"after": "canary"}` reads the
  trail and takes the most recent entry for the same capability and stage. No
  trail, no entry, a failure, a missing digest, or a different manifest digest
  escalates.
- **The chain verifies the trail first.** A tampered entry cannot unlock the next
  stage, even if it contains a pass.
- **Circular policies are rejected** at validation, not left as an unsatisfiable
  runtime state.
- **The trail records the chain.** Each entry carries `prior` (stage, seq, hash,
  outcome), so a later reviewer can walk the decision chain.
- **Continuity is scoped by capability.** A manifest change in another capability
  no longer escalates this one.
- **Examples and the demo.** The four example capabilities and `demo plane`
  chain production after canary; the demo prints `prior=canary#0` and
  `deploy_allowed=True`.

## Operational note

Carry the **same** trail artifact between stages. Two separate trails are two
separate histories, and the chain check escalates rather than guessing which one
is real. Keep the trail outside the agent's write access and anchor its head.

## Evidence

- 418 tests, lint clean, CI on Python 3.10–3.13.
- `assaystone demo plane` shows shadow pass/fail, canary approval, a chained
  production pass, and tamper detection.
- No user source, SQL, session content, or credentials are included.

## Known limits

- The chain proves a recorded sequence, not who wrote it. Authenticity still
  depends on where the trail lives and on anchoring its head.
- A local trail is single-writer; concurrent appends are not synchronized.
- The rest of the 0.6.0 limits remain: unsigned approvals are files, empty
  sessions escalate, unsupported languages escalate, and the Goodhart semantic
  family is still 0% resistance.
